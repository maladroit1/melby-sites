The CER Dog Lab

Compare two amazing African dogs — the Basenji and the Azawakh — and learn to write a Claim → Evidence → Reasoning essay.
This is your lab notebook: you type the ideas, and it helps you shape them.

Step 1 — What is C.E.R.?

Every good argument has three parts. Read the cards, then try the quick game.

Claim

What you think is true. Someone could argue with it.
🐕 “The Basenji is built for the rainforest.”

Evidence

The facts that back up your claim. Nobody argues with a fact.
🐕 “The Basenji has a short coat and a curled tail.”

Reasoning

The bridge: it explains why the evidence proves the claim. Start it with “because…”
🐕 “…because a short coat keeps a dog cool in a hot, wet forest.”

🎯 Quick game: Fact or Claim?

A fact can be looked up. A claim is something you have to prove. Tap your answer.

Step 2 — The 3-Color Trick

Writers color-code their sentences to make sure every part is there. Read each sentence and tap whether it's a Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning.

Step 3 — Pick Your Big Idea (Thesis)

A compare essay needs a spine — one main idea you're proving. Pick the angle you like best. (You can reword it later.)

    👋 For grown-ups (coaching tips)
    • Praise the “because,” not the fact. Finding a fact is easy; the reasoning bridge is the real skill.
    • Have her say the reasoning out loud before typing it. Talking it out beats staring at the page.
    • Use the colors as a checklist: a body paragraph with no purple has no reasoning yet.
    • The “✓ verify” tags are on purpose — checking a fact before using it is part of the assignment.
