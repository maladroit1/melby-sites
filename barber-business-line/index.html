💈 Your Own Business Text Line

A second phone number on the phone you already have — so client texts come from your business number, and your personal number stays personal. About 30–45 minutes total.

1Get a second number (eSIM)

Modern iPhones (XS/XR and newer) can hold a second phone line with no physical SIM card — it's called an eSIM, and you set it up right from the phone.

  1. Download the carrier's app (or use their website) and sign up for their cheapest plan with unlimited texting. Choose eSIM, and pick a local area code.
  2. Follow their activation steps — the phone will ask to "Add eSIM." Say yes.
  3. Go to Settings → Cellular. You'll now see two lines. Tap the new one and label it "Business."
Check first: if the phone is financed/locked through a carrier, it may need to be paid off or unlocked before a second carrier's eSIM will activate. One quick call to her current carrier answers this.

2Point texting at the Business line

  1. Settings → Cellular → Default Voice Line — keep this as Personal (so normal calls/texts don't change).
  2. In Settings → Apps → Messages → iMessage, make sure both numbers are enabled.
  3. When you start a new text, tap the line shown near the top of the compose screen and pick Business. iOS remembers the line per conversation after that.
Good news: replies from clients come back to the Business line automatically, in the same Messages app — just tagged with the Business label.

3Get the client list into the phone

Your list is in Excel. Two easy paths:

  • Easiest: the blast apps in Step 4 can import a CSV file directly. In Excel (or the free Numbers app on the iPad), open the file and export/share it as CSV, then save it to the Files app on the phone (AirDrop works great for this).
  • Alternative: add clients as regular Contacts (slower, but then they're named in Messages forever).

Keep two columns: First Name and Phone. That's all the apps need to personalize.

4Install a blast app & send

These apps send individual, personalized texts (not an awkward group chat), through the phone's own Messages app — which means they go out on whatever line the conversation uses.

  • Hit Em Up (~$5–10) — import CSV, write one message with {FirstName}, it queues each text; you tap Send on each. A 150-person list takes ~10 minutes.
  • Reach — Group Text & SMS — same idea, slightly different interface.

The one manual step that matters: before starting the blast, send one new text to yourself from the Business line so it's the "active" line, and spot-check the first 2–3 blast messages to confirm they show the Business number at the top. Then rip through the rest.

Sample message: "Hi {FirstName}! It's Sam — I'm moving to [New Shop] on [Street] starting [Date]. Same cuts, new chair 💈 Book by text right here at this number. Reply STOP if you'd rather not get updates."

5Keep it clean (2-minute rules)

  • ✓ Always say who you are in the first text.
  • ✓ If anyone replies STOP or "don't text me," take them off the list — for good.
  • ✓ Space it out: send in batches of ~30–50 with short breaks, not 300 in one burst — carriers can flag sudden floods as spam.
  • ✓ Put the Business number on your Instagram bio / booking page so new clients use it too.

🗓 What this costs & gets you

~$10/month + a ~$7 app. In return: a permanent business number clients can text to book, that follows you to any shop, forever — and your personal number never gets handed out again. When texts slow down someday, cancel the plan (but note: canceling gives up the number).

iPad note: texting from a phone number is an iPhone thing — do steps 1–4 on the phone. The iPad is great for cleaning up the Excel list.
